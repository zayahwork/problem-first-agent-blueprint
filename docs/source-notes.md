# Source notes and interpretation

## Intellectual starting point

This blueprint draws on user-supplied discussions attributed to Nes / `@nestharus` and a supplied work titled *Problem-First AI Engineering*. Useful ideas include diagnosing a problem before choosing a solution, preserving values and reasoning across work, deriving observers from material risks, and negotiating interactions among specialist objectives.

A later user-supplied adaptation, *The Problem-First Founder, Part One*, helped express those ideas in business terms. The reviewed installment contains completed Chapters 1 through 4 and a plan for later chapters. Planned chapter titles are not evidence that their full methods were present in that installment.

These sources were supplied privately. This repository does not reproduce their books, private discussions, or company records. It is an independently written generic synthesis. No author endorsement, exclusivity, or claim about who first invented these ideas is implied.

## Design choices in this repository

The concrete role names, record fields, state transitions, fingerprint requirements, reconciliation rules, tool mapping, and adoption tests are proposed implementation choices. They should be challenged against the actual environment and revised when evidence warrants it.

Quorum is one coordination pattern for unresolved material conflicts. It is not a requirement to keep many agents debating continuously. A global objective does not require universal numeric weights; use explicit priorities when quantities cannot be responsibly compared.

## What has and has not been established

This edition packages operating guidance, portable skills, and examples. It does not certify a deployed system, demonstrate unattended recovery, report a benchmark, or guarantee financial results, quality, context savings, or compute savings.

New implementations should keep evidence of their own trials. Attribute claims to the tested version and environment, distinguish observations from hypotheses, and preserve negative and inconclusive outcomes.
