# Example: investigate a lead handoff

This fictional walkthrough illustrates the records and decisions. It contains no measured results or universal targets.

## 1. Preserve the request

An operator says, "Leads are falling through. We need a full CRM and automatic follow-up."

That report supports the existence of a concern. Possible causes include missing intake records, unclear ownership, unavailable service capacity, poor fit, or follow-up performed elsewhere. The team has not established which cause matters most.

The decision is whether a small change in ownership can help suitable inquiries progress, or whether another constraint needs attention first.

## 2. Choose a bounded investigation

The coordinator asks an investigator to trace an owner-approved sample of recent inquiries across existing intake, contact, and appointment records. The company provides authorized access and an accountable operator. The investigator reports observations, conflicting records, and uncertainty.

The initial work is complete when the sample is reconciled to the declared standard or the investigation explains why a material gap prevents a conclusion. The broader commercial question can remain open.

## 3. Observe what matters

For eligible inquiries, the operator checks whether a responsible person accepted the next action. The observer compares the assignment record with evidence from the actual communication channel.

Missing records produce an unknown state. A known missed handoff produces an exception for the responsible owner. The team selects response targets and observation windows from the actual business process.

## 4. Resolve a conflict

Suppose the acquisition specialist proposes widening qualification and the delivery specialist objects because capacity is constrained. Their proposals affect the same shared rule.

They submit positions against the same evidence snapshot. Each explains expected gains, possible harm, uncertainty, and evidence that would change their view. Existing priorities may already resolve the tradeoff. Otherwise, the accountable decision owner sets a bounded exchange and obtains the material perspectives.

If a rule moved strict → broad → strict under unchanged relevant evidence, the coordinator would surface a possible cycle. If the return to strict was an authorized incident rollback or relied on new capacity evidence, it would be examined as that distinct case.

The participants do not vote a business objective into existence. The owner decides the tradeoff within their authority or commissions a smaller evidence-gathering step. A missing perspective or expired deadline cannot become an approval by silence.

## 5. Resume without repeating an effect

A worker prepares a draft internal reminder and yields with its artifact version and next step. A fresh worker can continue from that checkpoint.

If an authorized provider action were later sent and the response were lost, the action would be unknown. The system would reconcile the provider outcome before retrying. The issue tracker cannot safely infer failure from the missing response.

## 6. Evaluate separately

After a pilot, record:

1. Whether the assigned process behaved correctly.
2. Whether the operator used it without excessive parallel work.
3. Whether suitable inquiries progressed, with the comparison and its limitations.
4. Whether evidence supports another business commitment.

More reminders or booked calls alone would not establish profit. A verified software task can close while an outcome review waits for results. This preserves useful progress without overstating what the project established.
