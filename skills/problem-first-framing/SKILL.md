# Problem First Framing

Turn a request into the smallest problem definition that can guide a useful decision. Scale the diagnosis to consequence: a reversible edit may need a short paragraph; an expensive commitment may need a durable record.

## Frame the decision

1. Recover the user's desired outcome, constraints, existing decisions, and authorized scope. Treat the initial claim seriously while checking whether evidence supports its causal explanation.
2. Separate observations, proposed causes, constraints, desired outcomes, and proposed solutions. Identify who experiences the problem, when it occurs, and what consequence matters to them.
3. Consider a small set of materially different explanations. Include a simpler workflow or no new feature when that is plausible; do not invent alternatives merely to fill a template.
4. Compare evidence for and against the leading explanations. State source, relevant time window, and uncertainty. Missing evidence is an unknown, not evidence of absence.
5. Identify the missing fact most likely to change the decision, then use available evidence before asking the user for it.
6. Write a solution-neutral problem statement and recommend the smallest feasible probe that distinguishes the leading explanations.

Describe value in terms the user has established, such as saved time, reliability, avoided loss, revenue, or improved decisions. Mark any suggested priorities as proposals. Do not invent business goals, numeric targets, or a causal claim to make the frame appear complete.

## Make the next step useful

For a probe, specify the question, evidence source, affected population or workflow, observation window, expected discriminating signal, responsible actor, and stop condition. Distinguish existing owner-accepted thresholds from proposed thresholds. A comparison or baseline must be identified and available, not assumed.

Preserve the user's choice and authorization. A diagnosis request authorizes investigation and recommendations; it does not authorize implementation or external actions. If implementation is already requested and the uncertainty can be handled safely within scope, continue with a reversible step. Pause only the work that requires an unresolved material decision or additional authority.

Avoid turning routine work into a compulsory discovery exercise. End when the frame is sufficient for the next decision, or clearly state the remaining uncertainty and the smallest way to resolve it.
